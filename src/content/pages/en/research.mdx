My research lies at the interface between specialised clinical psychiatry and
clinical neuroscience. It aims to better characterise severe,
treatment-resistant and clinically complex psychiatric disorders, by developing
approaches that make symptoms objectively measurable, that allow clinical
trajectories to be analysed, and that improve the prediction of treatment
response. This work is carried out within the
[INTERACTIONS](https://www.interactions-lab.fr/) team, accredited by INSERM,
part of the [Lille Neuroscience & Cognition](https://lilncog.univ-lille.fr/)
laboratory. It draws on national and international collaborations, which have
given rise to more than 270 publications.

## Research themes and projects

<h3 id="calypso">Objective clinical psychiatry — CALYPSO</h3>

[CALYPSO](https://calypso.univ-lille.fr/) — *Clinique et Ana**LY**ses
**PS**ychiatriques **O**bjectives*, objective psychiatric assessment and
analysis — is a research programme developing an objective, computational
approach to clinical psychiatry. It draws in particular on artificial
intelligence methods, including computer vision, to contribute to the objective
measurement of psychiatric symptoms, the analysis of their evolution over time,
and the prediction of treatment response.

#### Fundamental clinical science

CALYPSO grew out of work and reflection on the notion of *fundamental clinical
science* — the study of the foundations on which the study of clinical
manifestations rests. Looking closely at the definitions and origins of signs
and symptoms in psychiatry, one quickly sees that the clinical model of
psychiatry developed, in France at least, on the model of medical semiology
within the anatomo-clinical method. That method assumes a causal relationship
between lesions observed post mortem and clinical manifestations. Yet the
anatomo-clinical premise does not appear to hold for psychiatric disorders. The
epistemological foundations of clinical psychiatry may therefore look fragile.

#### Psychiatric symptoms that can be measured

Technological developments allow certain psychiatric symptoms to be approached
differently. From this perspective, many psychiatric signs and symptoms have
been shown to be observable and measurable. Depression, for instance, is
characterised by reduced physical activity, reduced vocal pitch variation, and
diminished facial expressiveness and head movement.

#### Who was Calypso?

In Greek mythology, Calypso — from the Ancient Greek Καλυψώ, "she who conceals",
"she who hides" — is a sea nymph who kept Odysseus in her cave, out of love, for
seven years. The idea behind CALYPSO is to reveal and make visible clinical
manifestations that had until now remained hidden.

### Catatonia

A substantial part of my research is devoted to catatonia, understood as a
transdiagnostic psychomotor syndrome. This work draws on complementary
approaches, from clinical and epidemiological analysis through to neuroscience,
with particular attention to the underlying mechanisms, the identification of
clinical profiles, and the prediction of treatment response.

### Mechanisms of physical treatments

My research also addresses the mechanisms of action of physical treatments, in
particular electroconvulsive therapy and neuromodulation techniques, considered
both as major therapeutic tools in treatment-resistant presentations and as
models for understanding the mechanisms of clinical response.

## Directing the F2RSM Psy

Since February 2026 I have directed the [F2RSM Psy](https://www.f2rsmpsy.fr/) —
the regional federation for research in psychiatry and mental health of the
Hauts-de-France region. Established in 2007, this public interest grouping now
brings together 50 healthcare institutions with psychiatric activity across the
region, whether public, private, or private non-profit.

Its mission is to promote research in psychiatry and mental health across the
region: to facilitate research by creating the conditions for its development,
to foster research momentum arising from clinical practice, and to reflect on
care needs and on the evaluation of innovative practice.
